*Friday, January 29, 2027, 8:10 a.m.*

The support queue was on Derek's left monitor. On the right, his proposal had a question and no answer under *Cost*.

He sat at the office desk with the notebook beside the keyboard, open to the old benchmark settings. Tolliver wanted the proposal by nine. Yesterday he had forbidden another run or move of the file. Derek couldn't book a test now, even if he offered to pay for it himself.

The four private jobs he'd charged to QA were still under review. He had submitted the correction; Tolliver hadn't decided what to do about it. Whatever Derek could reserve for customer work, he had no permission to use it for this.

Priya's message appeared in Teams on the docked laptop.

*Available for a check before you send?*

He replied that he could join at 8:15. Until then he stayed in the queue. When the time came, he lifted the headset from the desk, opened their arranged video call and enabled his microphone and camera. Priya's video appeared. He selected the proposal window in the sharing controls and started sharing it.

“Startup, then a fixed diagnostic,” he said. “Same settings as the recorded runs. I want to compare what happens now.”

“Compare which part?”

“Loading. Then a small set of the benchmark questions.”

“And if the score's different?”

He looked down at the two dated tables in the notebook. The second test had scored higher. The weights file had matched both times. He had spent four reservations trying to understand results he still couldn't explain.

“I'd know we hadn't reproduced the earlier result.”

“You wouldn't have reproduced the earlier test. You'd have run a sample.”

“Right.”

“Do you know what compiled state survived the maintenance?”

“No. The stored file matched when I checked it Monday. I haven't run it.”

“Let's find out whether it starts before you promise him an explanation.”

The proposal's question still said *Reproduce January 14th and January 16th results*. He had put both dates there because leaving either out seemed like conceding something.

He selected the sentence and replaced it.

*Can the retained copy load using the recorded configuration, and what startup and diagnostic behavior can we record?*

Priya read it.

“That's a first test,” she said.

“It doesn't answer why the numbers changed.”

“No. I'd like to know that too. We need something we can run first.”

He took the pen from beside the notebook and wrote *startup first* below the settings. The old scores stayed on the page. He would use the settings as a reference, not describe the notebook as a current control.

“I'll ask Tolliver to put this one through support,” he said. “They've agreed to keep the file. Finding out whether it runs ought to be useful to them.”

“Put that in. He needs to know what he's getting for our time.”

Derek added the company purpose beneath the question. Below it he offered to save the startup output, commands and new files in company records, with access for Priya. If loading failed, they would still have that record to send Tolliver.

“And make it one attempt,” she said. “If it crashes, we look at the failure before you book another.”

He added that to the proposal.

With the share still limited to the document, he opened the reservation interface on the other monitor and chose the estimate view. Two GPUs, two hours. He entered the duration and requested the preview.

The all-in estimate was twelve dollars. Four allocated GPU-hours. The booking button was below it.

He copied the quoted ceiling into the proposal and left the button alone.

“That part's cheap,” he said.

“The two hours still have to come from somewhere.”

His queue was on the other screen. He had been thinking about what would fit in the reservation, not who would handle their tickets while they used it.

“I'll ask for Monday morning. Ten until noon.”

“I'll look at it with him,” Priya said. “Let's see what he says.”

He stopped sharing, said goodbye and left the call. The headset went back on the desk. He completed the proposal with the retained path, the saved loader, a fixed diagnostic sample and the recording and stop requirements. Under payment, he entered the requested company support allocation.

At 8:44 a.m. he attached the document to a new company email addressed to Tolliver, copied Priya and sent it. The sent item contained the proposal. Sixteen minutes remained before the deadline.

In a separate message to Tolliver, he asked for available transfer and home-endpoint records from his two failed attempts to be preserved through the appropriate owner. He had deleted the landed files. He did not know what records remained, and he asked for no new transfer.

At 8:52 a.m. a meeting invitation arrived for 11:10 with Tolliver and Priya. Derek opened it and accepted.

There was still no answer to the proposal. He brought the queue forward and opened the next ticket.

*Friday, January 29, 2027, 11:10 a.m.*

Derek was seated at the office desk when he opened the accepted invitation. He put on the headset, joined Teams and enabled his microphone and camera. Tolliver and Priya appeared in separate video tiles.

He selected the proposal window and started sharing.

“I'd like to run the startup test on Monday, with Priya,” he said. “I've put the time and estimate underneath.”

“I've read it,” Tolliver said. “Before we get to Monday, I want to be clear why we're having this conversation.”

Derek took his hand off the mouse.

“You ran private tests against QA. You tried to take the file home. I found out after you'd done both.” Tolliver spoke slowly, looking toward the camera. “I need to decide whether I'm letting you work on it again.”

“That's what I'm asking.”

“And you're asking for Priya's time as well as yours. What does the company get from this?”

“A check of the file we're keeping. Whether it starts, what resources it uses, what it writes. Right now I can tell you what it did two weeks ago. I can't tell you what would happen if we loaded it Monday.”

“You wanted to explain the benchmark scores yesterday.”

“I still do. This is smaller.”

He moved the pointer to the revised question. He would have liked to put the scores in front of Tolliver again. They were why he wanted another run, but they hadn't made the four earlier jobs company work.

“If it won't load, we'll send you the errors. You can decide whether it's worth doing anything else with it.”

Tolliver looked down for a moment.

“That's useful,” he said. “I can give you the two hours for that. I can't have this taking both of you away from work whenever you find another question.”

“Ten until noon on February 1st,” Derek said. “I'll stop at noon even if we're still loading.”

“Priya, can you cover that?”

“Monday morning, yes.” She looked toward her screen. “Derek, give me access to the saved record as well as the call. If something goes wrong, I want to be able to open it while you're dealing with the job.”

“Yes.”

“And if I say stop, stop. We can argue about the result afterward.”

Derek nodded. She was offering to spend Monday helping him get it running. He had been expecting her to refuse.

“I'll stop,” he said.

Tolliver asked him to scroll down to the working area.

“This needs its own access request?”

“Yes. The source stays read-only. Startup may need to compile again, so it needs somewhere else to write.”

“Send the service request. Get the resource owner to confirm the access and the space before you book.”

Priya leaned toward her camera.

“Include the recording and abort controls in that request. We need to check them before loading.”

Derek took the pen from beside the notebook and wrote *test the abort before the job* below the ceiling. He added *one job*, then *including failure*. A loading error would use up the permission too. He couldn't spend the rest of the morning trying again.

“I'll put the terms in a reply on the proposal thread,” Tolliver said. “Charge this to support with its actual purpose. The four QA entries are still a separate decision.”

Derek left the correction closed. He had hoped Tolliver might say something about it once they had agreed on Monday. The manager was looking down, and the call was quiet. Derek stopped sharing and left the proposal open on his right monitor.

He turned back to the notebook. Two hours might give him a running model and still leave the changed scores unexplained. Priya had asked on Wednesday how he would pay for more testing. His personal project code existed, but he had put no money into it. He had already told her he might have to borrow. He had no amount, no repayment plan and no usable copy at home to test instead.

“Would you consider a comparison after this?” he asked Tolliver.

Tolliver looked up.

“Bring me the result first. If there's a company reason for another test, we'll discuss it. If you want to pursue your own research, you'll need to fund it.”

Derek put the pen down. He wanted to know what that would cost before Theo asked. He had wanted to know before Priya asked too, and had gone on without finding out.

The email notification arrived on his laptop at 11:43 a.m. He brought the company client forward and opened Tolliver's reply. Priya was copied.

*Approved for one characterization job on Monday, February 1st, 10 a.m. to noon local time, using /ns/dvoss-isolated/v9 as a read-only source on Meridian. Maximum two GPUs, four allocated GPU-hours and the quoted $12 ceiling, charged to the company support allocation under my approval. Priya must witness the job and have independent access to its records.*

*Confirm resource-owner access, availability, a separate approved working area, independent recording and effective abort/time limits before booking or launch. Retain the job allocation and start/end records, output and errors, commands, working-area writes and generated scripts. Use the saved loader and the specified diagnostic only. A new script may be retained as output; execution requires Priya's inspection of the exact file for that scope. No training, child jobs, network tasks, customer inputs or extra resources.*

*Stop for loss of recording, out-of-scope execution or writes, a source-modification attempt, Priya's abort request or any ceiling reached. Preserve the failure record and copy results to Priya and me. No retry or second session without another permission. The existing hold remains except for this job and its recorded working-area writes: no source moves, deletion or external circulation. The past allocation correction remains a separate decision.*

Derek read the window and allowance once more. There was no permission to run that afternoon, or to use Monday's unused minutes on Tuesday.

“I can work with that,” he said.

He had one more question before sending the reply.

“Theo asked me about the public release this week. My roommate. I let him think I'd just been reading about it.”

Priya was still on the call. He kept his eyes on Tolliver's tile.

“I want to tell him what I've been doing. Including the unauthorized work.”

“Are you planning to show him anything?”

“Just talk. He ought to know before I spend more evenings on it. Or start looking at loans.”

Tolliver nodded.

“You can talk about your own conduct and spending. Keep the artifact, company records, private measurements, paths and customer details here.”

“I understand.”

“And your preservation request,” Tolliver said. “I've routed it to the appropriate owner. I don't have confirmation that those endpoint records were retained.”

Derek wrote *preservation pending* in the notebook. He had asked for someone to look; he did not know whether there was anything left to preserve.

At 11:47 a.m. he sent his acceptance on the thread. Then he said goodbye, left the meeting and put the headset on the desk.

The Monday window was in the received email. It was not on the job board.

*Friday, January 29, 2027, 6:40 p.m.*

Derek came from the office and stopped at the kitchen counter. Theo was eating at the secondhand table. A pot of rice stood on the stove with the serving spoon beside it.

“I'm in early tomorrow,” Theo said. “If you're planning one of those nights.”

“I'll keep it quiet.”

“Quiet includes talking at the computer.”

“I know.”

Derek took a bowl from the cupboard and a spoon from the drawer. He served rice from the pot, returned the serving spoon beside it, and carried his bowl and spoon to the table. The bowl was warm against his palm. He set it down, pulled out a chair and sat.

The notebook and laptop were still in the office. He had left them there.

“When you asked me about the model people were downloading,” he said, “I left something out.”

Theo looked up.

“I'd already been testing a private model I found at work. Before the public release.”

“You found it?”

“Yes. I didn't put that download up. I don't know whether it's a version of the one I found.”

The public file had a different published digest from the private one. The similar scores hadn't settled their relationship. He had no new answer to give Theo about that, and no permission to share the measurements.

“So you were doing this already when I asked if it was real?”

“Yes.”

Theo set his spoon in his bowl.

“Why didn't you say that?”

“I'd been doing it without permission.”

He could hear the kitchen refrigerator now. Theo hadn't said anything.

“I used company computer time,” Derek said. “Put it down as testing for work. It was my investigation. I've submitted a correction for those charges, and my manager's reviewing it.”

“Derek.”

“I know.”

“Are you going to lose your job?”

“I still have it. He's stopped me doing any more testing on my own. I have permission for one supervised test on Monday.”

“But he hasn't decided about the charges?”

“No.”

Theo looked at him across the table. Derek had spent the afternoon relieved about Monday. Saying the rest aloud made it difficult to expect Theo to feel the same way.

“I tried to get a copy home too,” he said. “That didn't work. I don't have one I can use here.”

“What made you want it that much?”

Derek rubbed his thumb along the bowl's warm rim.

“I found something I couldn't explain. Then I got it running, and I still couldn't explain it. I wanted to be the person who figured it out.”

“Do you get paid for figuring it out?”

“No. I wasn't asked to do it.”

Theo waited while he took a mouthful of rice.

“The test on Monday is work,” Derek said after he swallowed. “They want to know whether we can still get it running. They pay for those two hours. After that, if it's just me wanting answers, I'd have to ask to keep going and pay for it myself.”

“How much?”

“Monday's computer time is twelve dollars. I don't have a price for what I'd want to do afterward.”

Theo picked up his spoon.

“You can manage twelve dollars.”

“Yes. That isn't why I've been thinking about borrowing. I've already used four test reservations and I still have questions. I don't know how many more it would take, or what I'd need to rent.”

The spoon stopped above Theo's bowl.

“Borrowing from who?”

“I haven't asked anyone. I haven't settled an amount or payments. I mentioned it to Priya Wednesday.”

“Before you mentioned it to me.”

“She asked how I'd pay.”

“So am I.”

Derek set his spoon beside the bowl. He had brought up the loan because he meant to be honest this time. Theo had asked the next ordinary question, and he couldn't answer it.

“I need to price the next test. If there is one.”

Theo lowered his spoon.

“I thought you were reading about this stuff after work. Now you're telling me your boss is looking at charges, and you're thinking of a loan. Give me a minute.”

Derek waited. Theo ate some rice, then looked back at him.

“I'm not saying you can't spend your money on it. But leave your rent and utilities out of whatever you can afford. I can't cover your share because a test cost more than you expected.”

“I'm not asking you to.”

“I know. Work it out before you borrow.”

“I will.”

“And I do have to sleep. Especially when I'm going in early.”

Derek glanced toward the office doorway. He hadn't been planning to run anything tonight. That was an easy promise to make while Tolliver had already forbidden it.

“I'll keep the late calls and tests out of your work nights,” he said. “If there's something I can't schedule that way, I'll talk to you before I commit to it.”

“Okay.”

Theo took another mouthful. Derek picked up his spoon and ate too. He had expected more questions about the model. Theo was looking at the bowl in front of him.

“I should've told you when you asked,” Derek said.

“Yeah.”

“I didn't know enough to explain it.”

Theo looked up. “You could've told me you were working on it.”

Derek nodded. He had explained how to check a download while his own testing records were open on the other monitor. There had been plenty he could have said.

“I didn't want you asking whether I should be doing it,” he said. “I wanted to keep going.”

“I probably would've asked.”

“I know.”

For a while they ate without talking. Theo reached for the last rice in his bowl.

“What happens if Monday doesn't tell you much?”

“I look at what we got. Work out what another test would need.”

“Before looking at loans?”

“Yes.”

“And what can you afford to lose on it?”

Derek stayed in the chair. There was more rice in his bowl, and he had not answered the question.

“I haven't worked it out,” he said. “Not after rent and bills.”
