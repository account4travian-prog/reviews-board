# the author's voice calibration for Ghost Weights

**Authority:** the author's direct [Chapter 4 r5 rejection](../chapters/04-too-small/author-feedback-2026-09-26-r5.md), plus earlier dated author feedback cited by [the AI-ism field guide](ai-ism-field-guide.md). This note helps a reviewer notice mechanisms; it is not a word blacklist or a substitute for the author's ear. Do not put current-candidate highlights in a neutral cold-read bundle.

## What the r5 read exposed

The earlier guide defined AI-isms mainly as empty referents, maxims, `shape`, and unearned significance. the author's r5 notes reject a broader **voice move**: narration reaches for a compact, writerly explanation when Derek could simply report what he did, saw, or failed to understand. The line may have a referent and still sound synthetic.

| Mechanism to test | the author's r5 examples | Test before keeping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Decorative metaphor or personification | “failed at the door”; a word “held ... carefully”; formats that “assumed”; a spreadsheet that “taught” | Does the image make Derek's immediate experience clearer, or does it turn an ordinary fact into a quotable line? What literal event happened? |
| Moral or clever label on routine work | “dishonest version”; “honest death”; “cheap explanation”; “guess he hadn't earned” | Would Derek call it this, or is the narrator grading his method for the reader? State the method or error plainly. |
| Abstract compression and staged pivot | “stopped trying to open it and started trying to map it”; “This was a third thing”; “The word didn't fit anymore” | Can the reader point to a new action or consequence? If a preceding or following sentence already shows it, try cutting the summary. |
| Unspecified technical or lyrical word | `shape`, `fluent`, “read back raw,” “written down small” | What precise shape, output, byte operation, or capability does this mean for a returning reader? A technically legitimate term still needs context. |
| Dramatic interpretation of evidence | “Random damage didn't keep a schedule”; “awake in the dark” | Does the observation support the claim? Is this Derek's tentative thought or a narrator-certified conclusion? Does the line say more than the evidence? |

Items 6, 7, 8, 10, 21, and 24 in the source feedback also concern **time, terminology, point of view, or physical staging**. The cold reader and voice screener must not call all of them AI-isms. “Thursday” needs a clear reference to the prior week; “Theo's light was off” needs a readable room layout. These are reader comprehension failures even if the sentence has no synthetic flourish.

## What the r8 read exposed

the author's r8 notes (24 notes, 11 + 13, 2026-09-30) reject two further **voice moves**, both about the reader's ability to check Derek's work. Filed 2026-10-01 from the Q1 recall measurement (Council Session 6 D6–D12). These are **Tier-O review cues (exit 0)** — never blocking rejects, never reject strings.

| Mechanism to test | the author's r8 examples | Test before keeping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unexplained instrument language | "The queue took it and put it sixth in line"; "the tail sat still" / "the tail stayed still"; "It answered clean, on the new kernel"; "the ritual"; "suite's queue record" | Name the instrument, the literal action, and the reading. Chapter-private terms ("the tail", "the ritual", "the suite") need their meaning established on the page — what did Derek do, what did he see? Real-world tool names (sha256sum, nvidia-smi) don't need defining, but verbs attached to them still need the observation: "He checked squeue, the cluster's job listing" passes; "It answered clean" doesn't. |
| Conclusion stated without its evidence | "Tonight it passed."; "he looked at what the list would show anyone trying to reproduce his jobs"; "It answered clean, on the new kernel" | What observation would let the reader reach this conclusion independently? If the sentence gestures at evidence instead of naming it, the reader can't check the work. "Tonight it finished in eight minutes and forty-one seconds — the same weights, the same cards" passes; "Tonight it passed" doesn't. A verdict with its evidence quoted on the page ("the verdict: kept, superseding v7" after the log block) is fine. |

The r8 measurement also found a **gate-enforcement failure**: no voice screen ran on r8 before author feedback (D7: the r8 screen report is UNAVAILABLE). Recall on r8 is therefore unmeasured — any retrospective rescreen is COVERAGE, NOT PREDICTIVE RECALL. Standing rule from D12: every author-facing candidate saves its voice-screen report **before** author feedback.

## How to use the examples

First preserve an unprimed response to exact prose. Then, in the calibrated screen, inspect every paragraph for the *mechanism*, not just the quoted strings. Ask whether a plainer sentence would preserve the scene's useful information while sounding like Derek. Prefer deletion when the line only announces a conclusion already shown. Keep a vivid line if it has a precise referent, arises from Derek's perception, and changes the reader's understanding; do not ban metaphor categorically.

For r5 itself these examples are the answer key, so any scan after reading this note is retrospective. For a future candidate they are historical calibration. Measure the screener by new author feedback on that future candidate; until then, its recall outside these examples is unknown.
