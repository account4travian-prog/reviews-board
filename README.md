# Reviews Board — independent critique, run like a job queue

The critique arm of my fiction pipeline: a board where draft chapters are handed to reviewers who did not write them — other models in fresh contexts, and a standing auditor seat — and their findings come back as filed, checkable reports instead of chat opinions.

My role: I designed the board's rules and decide what happens to each finding. The reviewers report; the pipeline owner dispositions; I make the final call on anything that changes a chapter.

## How a review runs
- Each job is a folder: the text under review, the brief (what to look for, what not to touch), and the findings when they land.
- Reviewers work blind where it matters — a cold reader gets the text without the author's intent, because "did you follow the scene?" is a different question from "does the scene work?"
- Findings are specific: quoted line, the problem, and which class it belongs to. General impressions are not accepted as findings.
- Every finding gets a disposition — accepted, rejected with a reason, or deferred — and the disposition is part of the record.

## The record so far
16 job folders in the local mirror, 10 with completed findings. The board's value showed in the misses it caught that earlier passes had waved through — and in the findings I rejected, which are kept with their reasons, because a review system where the owner accepts everything is just a rubber stamp with extra steps.

The board is one seat of the [chapter pipeline](https://github.com/account4travian-prog/chapter-pipeline); the full grader stack is described there. Chapter texts stay out of this repo — it holds the method, the briefs, and the scorecards.
