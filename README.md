# Reviews Board: independent critique, run like a job queue

The critique arm of my fiction pipeline. Draft chapters go to reviewers who did not write them, other models in fresh contexts, and an auditor seat, and their findings come back as filed, checkable reports instead of chat opinions.

**My role:** I designed the board's rules and make the final call on anything that changes a chapter. The reviewers report; Atlas dispositions each finding; acceptance stays mine. Independence is structural, not asserted: a separate agent in a separate context, blind to the writer, the packet, and the revision history.

## How a review runs
- Each job is a folder: the text under review, the brief, and the findings when they land.
- A cold reader gets the text without the author's intent: "did you follow the scene?" is a different question from "does the scene work?"
- Findings are specific: quoted line, problem, class. General impressions are not accepted. Zero hits is a valid result, up to five substantiated findings; an honest empty report is signal.
- Every finding gets a disposition: accepted, rejected with a reason, or marked diagnostic-only.

## The record so far
16 job folders, 10 with completed findings. It caught a major character-knowledge defect in Ghost Weights chapter 13, where the chapter establishes a knowledge boundary and then crosses it at its climax. In chapter 16 it reported no substantiated draft findings and caught a defect in the job itself: the brief named the packet as the fidelity standard while the persona forbids reading it, and the registry excerpt was missing. The findings I rejected are kept with their reasons.

Reviewer and reader seats come from models I select and test through OpenCode: in September 2026 I commissioned timing runs across the free OpenCode models and staffed reader seats from that pool, and in October 2026 I directed seats to OpenCode models on my PC, including experimental and newly released models. Those runs were working comparisons (timing, read quality), not published benchmarks.

The board is one seat of the [chapter pipeline](https://github.com/account4travian-prog/chapter-pipeline); the full grader stack is described there. Chapter texts stay out of this repo, which holds the method, the briefs, and the scorecards.
